# Yasuko Mari — complete route

Soft, attentive and gently practical. Notices sleeves, uneaten food and tired eyes. Deflects attention by caring for the other person. Small hesitations reveal uncertainty; her humor is quiet. A first-year peer, never the nurse or the MC's mother.

Production labels in brackets identify score categories; hide them in the game. Read the setup, then the current relationship-temperature beat, one chosen response, and the shared closing. Do not display all alternatives to the player.

Relationship temperature is calculated BEFORE this scene: warm when raw good minus raw bad is at least 3; cool when it is at most -3; mixed otherwise. These lines change delivery, never unlock or skip scenes.

## mari.01 — The Spare Chair

**Location:** Infirmary · **Time:** 3-hour activity block · **Initial expression:** neutral

**Props:** public_leaflets, squeaky_chair · **Outfit:** school_uniform

### Scene

*[The nurse asks two first-years to prepare a public noticeboard outside the infirmary rest area. No patient records or medical tasks are involved.]*

**Mari:** You can use this chair. The other one makes a terrible noise.

**MC:** You know that from experience?

**Mari:** Everyone knows when someone uses it. That's the difficulty.

*[She moves her bag off the good chair and notices a loose thread on the MC's cuff.]*

**Mari:** Ano... your sleeve. It's caught on the button. May I?

**MC:** Oh. Yes, thanks.

**Narration:** She frees the thread, then steps back before I can feel that I'm being inspected.

**Mari:** Yasuko Mari. Mari is fine. The nurse said we're sorting the public leaflets today.

### Decision

#### 1. I'm Takahashi. Which pile should I take? [NEUTRAL]

**Mari:** The ones with the blue heading. They're easiest to mix up.

**MC:** I can probably manage that.

**Mari:** Good. If either of us gets confused, we can ask.

#### 2. You seem good at this. I'll let you do the careful parts. [BAD]

**Mari:** I can. But we were both asked to help, Takahashi-kun. Here—this pile is yours.

**MC:** I meant I don't want to get in your way.

**Mari:** Then take a pile. That would help more.

#### 3. Thanks for the sleeve. You don't have to look after me while we're working. [GOOD]

**Mari:** I wasn't—ah. I was, wasn't I? You caught me before the leaflets even started.

**MC:** I can keep track of my own buttons. Usually.

**Mari:** Then we'll trust you with the blue leaflets too.

### Continue

*[We sort the public information sheets and ask the nurse to check them. The chair squeaks once when the MC forgets which one to use.]*

**Mari:** That one.

**MC:** I understand the warning now.

**Narration:** She covers a small laugh with her hand, as if we're in a library rather than an office with very loud furniture.

## mari.02 — The Extra Lunch

**Location:** Canteen · **Time:** 3-hour activity block · **Initial expression:** neutral

**Props:** two_bento, thermos, paper_headings · **Outfit:** school_uniform

### Scene

*[We work on noticeboard headings during a long lunch/activity block. Mari has two cloth-wrapped lunch boxes.]*

**Mari:** I pack lunches for my siblings in the morning. Sometimes my hands keep going after I've counted everyone.

**MC:** So the second one is an administrative error?

**Mari:** A rather nice one, I hope. You may have it if you like.

*[She starts explaining every part of the meal before opening her own box.]*

**Narration:** Her tea is still sealed. I haven't seen her take a bite.

### Decision

#### 1. I'd like some. Let's both eat before the headings take over the table. [GOOD]

**Mari:** They do seem to be multiplying.

**MC:** I'll move the scissors. You rescue your lunch.

**Mari:** Thank you. I almost forgot it was getting cold.

#### 2. You should bring an extra one for me every day. [BAD]

**Mari:** Every day?

**MC:** If you already make too much.

**Mari:** I can offer you this one. I don't want that to become a promise I haven't made.

#### 3. Thanks, but I brought lunch. Could you save it for yourself later? [NEUTRAL]

**Mari:** I could. That hadn't quite occurred to me.

**MC:** Afternoons are long.

**Mari:** They are. I'll put it somewhere cool.

### Continue

*[We put the work aside for lunch, then finish the headings afterwards.]*

**Mari:** I used to be better at making small portions.

**MC:** You could practice on yourself.

**Mari:** That sounds oddly difficult when you say it. I'll try.

## mari.03 — The Tea She Likes

**Location:** Infirmary · **Time:** 3-hour activity block · **Initial expression:** peaceful

**Props:** thermos, two_tea_cups, drying_paper · **Outfit:** school_uniform

### Scene

*[The nurse permits a break at the visitor table while the noticeboard materials dry. Mari opens a thermos.]*

**Mari:** Barley tea. Mugicha. No caffeine, if that helps you decide.

**MC:** You carry an entire drinks menu in there?

**Mari:** Only the part people ask about most often.

*[She pours a cup for herself and pauses over the spare one.]*

**MC:** Is it your favorite?

**Mari:** Everyone at home likes it.

*[She answers automatically, then watches his face. Her fingers tighten slightly around the lid of the thermos.]*

**MC:** I asked whether you do.

### Decision

#### 1. Come on, that shouldn't be a difficult question. [BAD]

**Mari:** I heard the question. I was thinking.

**MC:** I didn't mean to put you on the spot.

**Mari:** Then please give me a moment.

#### 2. You can think about it. I'm not testing you. [GOOD]

**Mari:** I like the roasted smell. Especially before the first sip.

**MC:** That sounds like an answer.

**Mari:** It is, isn't it? Yes. I like it.

#### 3. I'll try a little, if there's enough. [NEUTRAL]

**Mari:** There is. I brought a spare cup.

**MC:** Of course you did.

**Mari:** I may be predictable, Takahashi-kun. I have other qualities, I promise. Give me a minute to remember them.

### Continue

*[We drink while the paper dries. Mari closes her eyes for a brief, unguarded breath over the warm cup.]*

**Narration:** It is the first thing I've seen her enjoy without first checking whether someone else needs it.

**Mari:** Better while it's warm. We should stop talking about it and drink.

## mari.04 — Ask the Nurse

**Location:** Infirmary · **Time:** 3-hour activity block · **Initial expression:** concerned

**Props:** noticeboard, staff_only_sign · **Outfit:** school_uniform

### Scene

*[We hang the noticeboard sheets. A classmate asks Mari to fetch medicine while the nurse is briefly in the adjoining office.]*

**Mari:** Please ask the nurse when she comes back. I can't give you that.

**Classmate:** But you know where everything is.

**Mari:** That doesn't make me qualified to use it.

*[The classmate waits outside the office. Mari checks that the noticeboard is straight, then checks it again.]*

**MC:** Does that happen often?

**Mari:** People see me here and assume I'm useful in every possible way.

**Narration:** She gives a small smile, but there is very little amusement in it.

### Decision

#### 1. We can put a staff-only label on that cupboard. [NEUTRAL]

**Mari:** The nurse has one. It might need to be more visible.

**MC:** Public information project finally finds its subject.

**Mari:** A useful discovery.

#### 2. You answered clearly. I'll back you up if they ask me. [GOOD]

**Mari:** Thank you. I don't need you to argue with them.

**MC:** Just say the same thing.

**Mari:** Yes. That would be enough.

#### 3. It would've been quicker to fetch it and let the nurse check later. [BAD]

**Mari:** No. The nurse needs to decide what is appropriate.

**MC:** I thought it was a small thing.

**Mari:** Then waiting for the right person is a small thing too.

### Continue

*[The nurse handles the request. We finish the public noticeboard and leave the medical cupboards alone.]*

**Mari:** I like helping here. I still want to be allowed to sit in the quiet chair sometimes.

**MC:** We should put that in the leaflet.

**Mari:** Perhaps not in quite those words.

**Narration:** It sounds easy until I notice her eyes return to the custard.

**If warm:**

**Mari:** You remembered I liked the roasted tea too. I wasn't expecting you to remember.

**If mixed:**

**Narration:** Her smile reaches her eyes before she lowers them to the list.

## mari.06 — The Button

**Location:** Class B · **Time:** 3-hour activity block · **Initial expression:** neutral

**Props:** sewing_kit, loose_button, fabric_backing · **Outfit:** school_uniform

### Scene

*[A class craft block becomes an opportunity to repair loose buttons on the fabric display backing. Mari has brought a small sewing kit.]*

**MC:** You look prepared for every possible fabric emergency.

**Mari:** Only ordinary ones. Please don't invent a difficult one.

*[The MC's own blazer button comes loose while he reaches for the thread. Mari immediately sets down her work.]*

**Mari:** I can do that before it falls off.

**MC:** What about your part of the backing?

**Mari:** I'll finish it afterwards.

**Narration:** There is already a small queue of repairs beside her.

### Decision

#### 1. Thanks. You really are everyone's mum here. [BAD]

**Mari:** Your classmate, Takahashi-kun. I'd like to stay your classmate.

**MC:** I meant you're reliable.

**Mari:** You can call me reliable. I would prefer that.

#### 2. It can wait until the backing's finished. [NEUTRAL]

**Mari:** Put the button somewhere safe if it comes off.

**MC:** Front pocket. I have a system.

**Mari:** Then I won't interrupt your system.

#### 3. Could you show me instead? I'd like to manage the next one myself. [GOOD]

**Mari:** Slowly. The stitches don't need to be perfect; they need to hold. And please don't look at me while you're holding the needle.

**MC:** A refreshing school assessment.

**Mari:** Give it here. I'll show you the first stitch; you can do the rest.

### Continue

*[With Mari's demonstration if he requested it, or the class sewing guide if he did not, the MC secures his own button. Mari returns to her own work. His stitches are noticeably less neat than the display backing.]*

**MC:** I think the button has character now.

**Mari:** It has a great deal of thread. We can call that character.

## mari.07 — The Message From Home

**Location:** Lobby · **Time:** 3-hour activity block · **Initial expression:** concerned

**Props:** phone, supply_box, folded_cloth · **Outfit:** school_uniform

### Scene

*[While we carry spare materials to the lobby, Mari receives a message asking her to collect groceries and help a sibling with homework that evening.]*

**Mari:** I should have expected that. It is their difficult subject this term.

**MC:** And you have your own assignment due.

**Mari:** I can do it later.

*[She shifts the box, trying to type an answer with her free thumb. The lid slides.]*

**MC:** Let me set this down first.

**Mari:** Sorry. Yes. Thank you.

**Narration:** She apologizes again when a folded cloth falls out, although nobody was hurt and nothing was damaged.

### Decision

#### 1. What can you realistically do tonight? You can answer with that, not the best possible version of you. [GOOD]

**Mari:** I can collect the groceries. The homework will need to be tomorrow, or someone else can help.

**MC:** That sounds clear.

**Mari:** It sounds strange. I'll try writing it before I change my mind.

#### 2. Can we finish the school task another day? [NEUTRAL]

**Mari:** We can ask the teacher. I'd rather not quietly leave it for you.

**MC:** Then we ask.

**Mari:** All right.

#### 3. They need you. Your assignment can wait one night. [BAD]

**Mari:** It already waited last night.

**MC:** I didn't know.

**Mari:** No. I haven't been saying so. That doesn't make the time appear.

### Continue

*[We finish the carry together. Mari sends a specific reply and receives a short acknowledgment. It doesn't solve every expectation at home, but tonight now has a limit.]*

**Mari:** I keep expecting a clear answer to sound unkind.

**Narration:** She reads her own message once more, then puts the phone away.

## mari.08 — The Chair Is for Sitting

**Location:** Infirmary · **Time:** 3-hour activity block · **Initial expression:** peaceful

**Props:** visitor_chairs, clean_blankets, book · **Outfit:** school_uniform

### Scene

*[The nurse tells the student volunteers to take a break before doing anything else. Mari reaches for an untidy stack of clean blankets anyway.]*

**Nurse:** Mari. Chair first. Those can wait.

**Mari:** I was only going to fold the top two.

**Nurse:** They will survive their current condition.

*[Mari sits. The MC takes the other visitor chair, the one that squeaks.]*

**MC:** I think the chair is pleased to be useful.

**Mari:** You don't have to keep me company if you have something to do.

### Decision

#### 1. I'll read quietly here until the break's over. [NEUTRAL]

**Mari:** All right. I won't ask what you're reading every thirty seconds.

**MC:** I'd appreciate that. I might not know yet.

**Mari:** A difficult book, then.

#### 2. You worry too much. I'll make sure you rest from now on. [BAD]

**Mari:** I don't need someone else deciding my whole day.

**MC:** You weren't going to stop by yourself.

**Mari:** I can accept a reminder without giving up every decision.

#### 3. Would you prefer company or a little quiet on your own? [GOOD]

**Mari:** Company. Quiet company, if that's... if that's all right. Stay where I can see you, but you don't have to keep talking.

**MC:** I can attempt that.

**Mari:** A brave promise, in that chair.

### Continue

*[The break ends when the nurse checks in. Mari is tired but well; there is no collapse or romanticized illness. They finish only the assigned public materials, not extra work.]*

**Mari:** I didn't fold a single blanket.

**MC:** A terrible afternoon for blankets.

**Mari:** They seem to have managed.

## mari.09 — Before the Bell

**Location:** Sports field / hardcourt · **Time:** 3-hour activity block · **Initial expression:** warm

**Props:** practice_mat, sports_bag · **Outfit:** aikido_gi_and_trousers · **Open-campus day only** · **Required block:** morning

**MC:** I didn't know you did martial arts.

**Mari:** I usually practice before the house gets busy. It helps me begin the day with one thing at a time.

**MC:** You're good at making it look calm.

**Mari:** You didn't see the mornings when I forget which foot I'm using.

*[The instructor calls for everyone to put away the mats. Mari carries one, leaving the others for the group.]*

**Narration:** She catches me noticing and almost smiles.

### Decision

#### 1. What do you enjoy about it when nobody needs you to be good at it? [GOOD]

**Mari:** The moment when the movement starts to feel continuous. I stop counting things I haven't done yet.

**MC:** I'd like to find something that does that for me.

**Mari:** You could try the beginner session. Nobody starts graceful.

#### 2. Remind me never to annoy you. You look harmless until you do that. [BAD]

**Mari:** I would rather you listened because I asked, not because you imagined I might hurt you.

**MC:** It was supposed to be a compliment.

**Mari:** Then tell me what you actually liked.

#### 3. Can I help carry that mat? [NEUTRAL]

**Mari:** There's another one by the fence. This one's light enough.

**MC:** Right. Separate jobs.

**Mari:** We appear to be learning.

### Continue

*[The workshop fills the block with ordinary beginner exercises. Mari makes a mistake in front of the group, laughs at herself and tries again.]*

**Narration:** For once, nobody seems to need anything from her except that she enjoy being here.

**Narration:** She has already said more than she seemed to expect. I don't ask her to prove the point again.

## mari.12 — Not Tonight

### Scene

*[During independent study, Mari tells the MC she asked her family to share two evening tasks. The first conversation was awkward.]*

**Mari:** I still feel guilty about leaving a blank space after my name.

**MC:** Those are things you can put on the page.

**Mari:** Yes. Specific things. I can ask for specific things.

**Mari:** I can keep track of what actually happens, instead of only what I hoped would happen.

**Mari:** I think so too.

#### 3. Your family sounds awful. You should stop helping them completely. [BAD]

**Mari:** I love them. I am trying to change something, not erase them.

**MC:** I don't like how much they ask of you.

**Mari:** Neither do I, sometimes. Please let the conversation be more complicated than that.

### Continue

*[They use the rest of the block for their own assignments. Mari writes her available evenings on the household page before closing the notebook.]*

**Narration:** Nothing is solved by a single conversation. At least the next one will begin with something she actually wants.
